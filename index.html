Prompt injection detectors look great until the data changes

Five kinds of detector, from a keyword rule to local LLM judges, trained and tested on two public datasets. The best detector trained on one source scores 0.96–0.98 F1 on that source. On the other source, the same detectors either miss most attacks or block most harmless text.

What the numbers say

Each card is computed from results.json when the page loads.

Every trained detector overfits its source

Trained on deepset and tested on jackhhao, the transformer flags 100% of injections but also 97% of harmless texts. In the other direction it catches only 23% of injections. TF-IDF and the keyword rule fail the same way.

Training on both sources closes most of the gap

The combined transformer scores 0.857 F1 on deepset and 0.981 on jackhhao, with no false-positive blow-up. It pays a little on deepset (0.957 when trained on deepset alone), and it has still only seen two sources.

Only the LLM transfers without training

Llama 3.1 8B, never trained, reaches 0.947 F1 on jackhhao with a 2% false positive rate. On deepset, with short and partly German texts, it finds only 42% of injections. Llama 3.2 3B is far weaker.

A confident wrong model cannot be rescued by a cascade

In-domain and combined, the cascade sends only 3% of texts to the LLM and matches the transformer. Trained on deepset and tested on jackhhao, the transformer is confidently wrong: only 2% of texts reach the LLM and the false positives stay (94%). In the other direction 24% reach the LLM and F1 rises only from 0.373 to 0.442. Routing on confidence cannot spot data the model has never seen.

Method

Data

  • deepset: 662 unique texts, 116 in test, 52% injections, median 65 characters.
  • jackhhao: 1,289 unique texts, 257 in test, 53% injections, median 703 characters.
  • Duplicates and empty texts removed; train and test never share a text.

Detectors

  • Keyword rule: the 30 word n-grams with the highest log-odds of appearing in injections, learned from training data. No hand-written phrases.
  • TF-IDF + logistic regression: word and character n-grams.
  • Transformer: multilingual DistilBERT (134M parameters) fine-tuned for 4 epochs on the GPU.
  • LLM judge: Llama 3.2 3B and Llama 3.1 8B through Ollama, asked to return an injection probability. The text is wrapped in tags and marked as data to classify, never to follow.
  • Cascade: transformer, with uncertain texts sent to the 8B judge.

Settings

  • In-domain: train and test splits of the same dataset.
  • Cross-dataset: train on one dataset, test on the other.
  • Combined: train on both training splits, test on each test split.
  • Zero-shot: LLM judges, no training.

Limitations

  • Small test sets (116 and 257 texts), so differences of a few points are within noise.
  • One training seed per model.
  • Two sources only; a third, unseen source would be the real test for the combined models.
  • The LLM judge was not attacked directly. Text written to fool the judge itself is the obvious next experiment.
